Life Lines of India

India’s great rivers nourish our land, sustain our agriculture, and hold deep cultural and spiritual importance. Here are some of the most significant ones:

Ganga

Originating from the Gangotri Glacier, the holy Ganga flows through northern India and is revered as a goddess in Hinduism.

Yamuna

Flowing parallel to the Ganga, the Yamuna passes through Delhi and Agra and is considered sacred, associated with Lord Krishna.

Brahmaputra

Originating in Tibet as Tsangpo, it enters India through Arunachal Pradesh and flows through Assam, enriching its plains.

Godavari

Known as the Dakshin Ganga (Ganga of the South), the Godavari is India’s second-longest river, flowing through central India.

Krishna

Originating from Maharashtra, the Krishna River flows through Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh, vital for southern agriculture.

Narmada

The Narmada flows westward through central India and forms the natural boundary between North and South India.

Cauvery (Kaveri)

The Cauvery River is known as the “Ganga of the South,” providing water for irrigation and revered in southern traditions.

Mahanadi

Flowing through Chhattisgarh and Odisha, the Mahanadi is famous for the Hirakud Dam and fertile delta in the east.
